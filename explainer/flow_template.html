The flow behind each phase

Pick a point on the phase diagram. The top right panel shows the theory's dynamics there as a flow, in a plane spanned by the activity of the antennal-lobe module (the connectome's leading eigenvector) and of the rest of the brain. The bottom row shows the real thing: the module's 526 neurons and their actual synapses, simulated live in your browser with the same stochastic Wilson–Cowan rules, and every run's activity over time.

What the symbols mean
Knobs (the axes of the phase diagram)
g
Coupling. One number that multiplies every synapse. Each synapse count is first divided by 742.7, the average number of input synapses per neuron.
r
Inhibition ratio. An inhibitory synapse (GABA, glutamate, histamine) counts as −r times an excitatory (acetylcholine) one.
State (the axes of the flow panel)
x
Module activity: the fraction of the 526 antennal-lobe neurons that are active. In the reduced model, activity takes the shape of the module's leading eigenvector, ρ_i ∝ v_i.
y
Rest-of-brain activity: the fraction of the other 166,690 neurons that are active.
Rates and spectrum
α
Switch-off rate of an active neuron: 1 per time unit (0.906 effective, because all neurons update together every Δt = 0.2).
λ_M
Leading eigenvalue of the module's excitatory wiring (5.54): how strongly the module amplifies its own activity.
R = gλ_M/α
The module's reproduction number: how many switch-ons one active module neuron causes, on average, before it switches off. Activity can grow only if R > 1, that is above g_lin = α/λ_M ≈ 0.163.
K_eff
Effective number of independent units in the module (about 55 without inhibition). Chance fluctuations scale like 1/√K_eff, so a small K_eff means the module is easily silenced by bad luck.
δ(r)
Extra saturation from inhibitory and excitatory inputs arriving at the same neuron at once inside the module (0 at r = 0). It lowers the module's active level without moving g_lin.
τ(g)
Mean time until the module's activity dies out by chance: ln τ ≈ K_eff·(ln R − 1 + 1/R). It grows exponentially with K_eff.
T
Observation time of the simulations: 3,000 time units.
e_MB, e_BM
Measured couplings: the mean input a module neuron gets from a fully active rest of the brain (4.96), and the reverse (0.0083).
λ_B(r)
Gain of the lumped rest of the brain, set so that it switches on at the mean-field delocalization line.
Phases
Quiescent
τ < 200: activity dies quickly.
Slow window
200 ≤ τ < T: activity lingers, then dies. This is the Griffiths-like window.
Localized
τ ≥ T: activity lasts, carried by fewer than N/4 neurons (participation ratio).
Delocalized
Activity spread over N/4 or more neurons.

How to read it

  • Quiescent (g < g_lin): every arrow in the flow panel points to the silent corner, and in the bottom row the module goes dark within a few tens of time units.
  • Slow window: a stable "module on" point has appeared (R > 1), so the arrows now lead to it. But the module has only about 55 independent units, and chance runs of switch-offs keep dropping it into silence. In the bottom row the thick and thin lines fall to zero one by one, at times spread over decades. This is the Griffiths-like slowness.
  • Localized active: the module's active level is deep enough that runs outlast T. The left and right antennal lobes stay lit while the rest of the brain is barely driven.
  • Delocalized: the rest of the brain sustains itself and the stable point moves to large y. The live view shows the module only, so this phase appears there as a saturated module.
  • Inhibition (r): slide r up and watch the module's active level drop and the window widen. Inhibitory neurons inside the module light up together with the excitatory ones they cancel.

The reduced model behind the flow panel

dx/dt = α[(R − 1)x − R(1 + δ(r))x²] + g·e_MB·y·(1 − x) R = g·λ_M/α dy/dt = α[(R_B − 1)y − R_B·y²] + g·e_BM·x·(1 − y) R_B = g·λ_B(r)/α

What is computed and what is a caricature. The phase diagram's curves come from the theory page: the onset from the branching eigenvalue, the window from the module's extinction time, the delocalization line from mean field. The module half of the flow uses the theory's numbers (λ_M, δ(r), K_eff). The rest of the brain is many small islands, not one population; here it is lumped into one variable. The live simulation is exact for the module's own synapses but ignores input from outside the module, which supplies about half of a module neuron's synapses.
